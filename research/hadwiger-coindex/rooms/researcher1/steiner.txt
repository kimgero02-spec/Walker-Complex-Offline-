                                                                                1. Introduction
                                             Given two finite graphs G and H, we say that G contains H as a minor if
                                          there exists a sequence of vertex-deletions, edge-deletions and edge-contractions (in
                                          arbitrary order) that transforms G into a graph that is isomorphic to H. Hadwiger’s
                                          conjecture, formulated more than 80 years ago by Hadwiger [9], is the major open
                                          problem in the theory of graph coloring, and arguably one of the most challenging
                                          open problems in all of combinatorics. It suggests a deep connection between the
                                          chromatic number of a graph and the containment of clique-minors.
                                          Conjecture 1 (Hadwiger 1943 [9]). Let t be a positive integer. Every graph G
                                          satisfying χ(G) ≥ t contains Kt as a minor.
                                             Hadwiger’s conjecture is known to hold for t ≤ 6, but remains widely open for
                                          any value t ≥ 7. It was proven for t ≤ 4 by Hadwiger himself [9] and independently
                                          by Dirac [5]. The case t = 5 was proven to be equivalent to the 4-Color-Theorem
                                          by Wagner [26], and also the case t = 6 was reduced to the 4-Color-Theorem in
                                          a landmark paper by Robertson, Seymour and Thomas [20] in 1993. Recently,
                                          there has been some exciting progress towards the linear weakening of Hadwiger’s
                                          conjecture, which states that there exists some absolute constant C > 0 such that
                                          every graph G with χ(G) ≥ Ct contains Kt as a minor [17, 4]. The current state
                                          of the art-result coming from these works is a theorem by Delcourt and Postle [4],
                                          stating that for some absolute constant C > 0 and t large enough every graph of
                                          chromatic number at least Ct log log t contains Kt as a minor. For more background

   A substantial qualitative strengthening of Hadwiger’s conjecture was proposed
in 1995 by Gerards and Seymour [10]. It states that, in any graph of chromatic
number at least t, not only should it be possible to find Kt as a minor, but in
fact one should be able to find Kt as a so-called odd-minor. Given two graphs
G and H, we say that G contains H as an odd-minor, if a graph isomorphic to
H can be obtained from a subgraph G′ ⊆ G by selecting an edge-cut in G′ and
contracting all the edges in this cut simultaneously. It is clear by definition that
whenever a graph G contains a graph H as an odd-minor, it also contains H as
a minor. However, odd-minors are significantly more restricted than minors, for
instance they preserve the parity of cycle-lengths. A good example to illustrate this
difference are the complete bipartite graphs: While they can contain huge cliques
as ordinary minors, none of them contains even K3 as an odd-minor, since the
containment of K3 as an odd-minor necessitates the existence of an odd cycle.
Conjecture 2 (Gerards and Seymour 1995 [10]). Let t be a positive integer. Every
graph G satisfying χ(G) ≥ t contains Kt as an odd-minor.
   Given that Conjecture 2 is a substantial strengthening of Hadwiger’s tremen-
dously challenging conjecture, it is not surprising that it has resisted all attempts
of resolution thus far. The cases t ≤ 4 of the conjecture were proven by Catlin [2],
and a proof of the case t = 5 (a strengthening of the 4-Color-Theorem!) was an-
nounced by Guenin in 2004, cf. [22], but still has not been made public. While
the precise form of the conjecture remains widely open for t ≥ 6, as for Hadwiger’s
conjecture, there has been progress regarding asymptotic results [8, 16, 18, 25], with
the current state of the art being a bound of O(t log log t) on the chromatic number
of odd Kt -minor-free graphs from [25].
Topological lower bounds. If, as many researchers in the community seem to be-
lieve, the precise or even asymptotic form of Hadwiger’s conjecture or its odd-minor
variant is false, then one should put efforts into constructing counterexamples. In
order to do so, it would be necessary to construct Kt -minor-free graphs of high chro-
matic number. But chromatic number is a surprisingly complex parameter, and we
do not really understand in depth what makes the chromatic number of a graph
large—in particular, approximating the chromatic number of an n-vertex graph to
within a factor better than n1−ε is computationally intractable [7]. This suggests
that one will have to rely on one of the few known lower bounds on the chromatic
number to construct such graphs. One of the simplest such lower bounds is the
clique number ω(G), but since a clique subgraph directly yields a clique minor of
the same order, it seems badly suited for the problem. A much stronger and more
general lower bound is the fractional chromatic number χf (G), that is obtained by
a linear programming relaxation of an integer program for the chromatic number.
In fact, for many graphs of large chromatic number that one typically encounters,
including random graphs, χf (G) will also be large and close to χ(G). However, the
hopes of disproving Hadwiger’s or the Odd Hadwiger’s conjecture using fractional
chromatic number as a lower bound are dampened by results of Reed and Sey-
mour [19] and Kawarabayashi and Reed [11], showing that any graph G satisfying
χf (G) > t − 1 contains a rather big clique, namely K⌊(t+1)/2⌋ , as an odd-minor.
If we cannot rely on fractional chromatic number as a lower bound to disprove
(Odd) Hadwiger’s conjecture, maybe we should consider lower bounds that work
orthogonally to the fractional chromatic number, in the sense that they can be large
on graphs with small fractional chromatic number. The most well-explored such
bounds are the topological lower bounds on the chromatic number. The probably

proof of Kneser’s conjecture by Lovász [13], showing that the chromatic number of

the chromatic number of Kneser graphs employed by Lovász to arbitrary graphs G

Definition 1 (Box complexes, cf. [14, 3]). Let G be a graph. The vertex-set of
both B(G) and B0 (G) is V (G) × {1, 2}. A subset X ⊆ V (G) × {1, 2} forms a

sides of a complete bipartite subgraph of G (the sides are allowed to be empty).
Similarly, a subset X ⊆ V (G) × {1, 2} forms a face of B(G) if it forms a face of
B0 (G) and additionally both the projections X1 , X2 of X have at least one common
neighbor in G.

   In the well-written and comprehensive surveys on this topic by Matoušek and
Ziegler [14] from 2003 and by Daneshpajou and Meunier [3] from 2023, these pa-
rameters are compared to each other and put into a hierarchy. In Figure 1 that is
emulated from the paper by Daneshpajou and Meunier, we display the currently
known topological lower bounds on the chromatic number and their relations.
Combinatorial parameters. Since the focus of the paper at hand is combina-
torial, and since it will not be necessary to understand our results or proofs, we
refrain from giving the detailed definitions of all the parameters displayed in the
figure, and instead focus on the two purely combinatorial ones, namely zig(G) and
cd(H). Detailed explanations of all parameters and background can be found in [3].
Definition 2 (Zig-Zag-number, cf. [3], Section 4.1). Let c : V (G) → N be a proper
coloring of a graph G. A zigzag of length k in the properly colored graph (G, c) is
defined as a sequence of vertices z1 , . . . , zk ∈ V (G) such that c(z1 ) < · · · < c(zk )
and such that the sets of vertices {zi |i odd} and {zi |i even} are complete to each
other in G, i.e., form the two sides of a complete bipartite subgraph.

   The zig-zag-number zig(G) of a graph is then defined as the minimum, over all
proper colorings c : V (G) → N of G, of the maximum length of a zigzag in (G, c).
   For most of the parameters in Figure 1 the proof that they provide a lower
bound on χ(G) relies on topology, and in most cases on the Borsuk-Ulam theorem.
However, one does not need topology to see that zig(G) ≤ χ(G) for every graph G,
since the latter follows directly by definition of a zigzag. Furthermore, note that
zig(G) is the strongest of all the lower bounds in Figure 1. Thus, zig(G) provides a
combinatorial tool to simultaneously bound all the known topological parameters
that provide lower bounds on χ(G).
Definition 3 (2-colorability defect, Kneser representation, cf. [14], page 8–9 and [3],
Section 4.1). Let H = (V, E) be a hypergraph. The Kneser graph KG(H) induced
by H is the graph with vertex-set E in which two hyperedges e1 , e2 are adjacent
if and only if e1 ∩ e2 = ∅. If G is a graph, we say that a hypergraph H is a
Kneser-representation of G if KG(H) is isomorphic to G.

     The 2-colorability defect of a hypergraph H = (V, E) is defined as3
    cd(H) := min{|U | : U ⊆ V such that (V \ U, {e ∈ E : e ∩ U = ∅}) is 2-colorable}.
   It is worth mentioning and not hard to prove that every graph admits at least
one (and in fact, many) Kneser-representations. Therefore, the following result,
a wide-ranging generalization of Lovász’ lower bound on the chromatic number of
Kneser graphs due to Dol’nikov [6] and Křı́ž [12], can be used as a lower bound for
the chromatic number of any graph G.
Theorem 1 (Dol’nikov [6], Křı́ž [12]). Let G be a graph with a Kneser-representation
H. Then χ(G) ≥ cd(H).

Our results. In this paper, we investigate how the hierarchy of topological lower
bounds on the chromatic number interacts with the presence of clique minors, and
thus evaluate the potential these lower bounds provide for attacking Hadwiger’s
conjecture or its odd-minor variant. The investigation of this topic was initiated
in previous work of Simonyi and Zsbán [24] and even earlier by Simonyi and Tar-
dos [23], where they investigate the relaxations of Hadwiger’s conjecture and its

odd-minor variant obtained by putting one of the topological lower bounds on the
chromatic number in place of χ(G).
   Regarding Hadwiger’s conjecture, Simonyi and Tardos (see Corollary 25 in [23])
observed that known results from the literature, in particular the so-called Kℓ,m -
theorem and the stronger Zig-Zag-theorem (relating to zig(G) defined above), imply
that every graph for which one of the topological lower bounds on the chromatic
number attains a value of at least t, contains K⌊t/2⌋,⌈t/2⌉ as a subgraph and therefore
K⌊t/2⌋+1 as a minor. Due to this observation, Simonyi and Zsbán [24] focused on the
stronger Odd Hadwiger’s conjecture and stated that “Our main concern here is that
the Odd Hadwiger’s conjecture looks much more difficult in this respect”. Indeed,
while the presence of a large clique minor can be directly deduced from the presence
of a large balanced complete bipartite subgraph, the latter does not at all imply
the existence of a large odd clique minor—as mentioned before, bipartite graphs
are odd-K3 -minor-free. In fact, for the right choice of parameters, graphs like the
Kneser graphs K(n, k), for which the topological lower bounds on the chromatic
number are tight, can exhibit arbitrarily large odd girth, meaning that it will not
be possible to find a bounded-size subgraph that provides a large odd clique minor.
This led Simonyi and Zsbán to explicitely pose the following open problem in [24],
which remained unsolved thus far:
Problem 1 (Problem in Section 5 of [24]). Does there exist an absolute constant
c > 0 for which the following is true? If a graph G satisfies zig(G) ≥ t, then G
contains an odd complete minor on at least ct vertices.
   As the main result of this paper, we solve the above problem in the affirmative,
with the constant c = 21 :
Theorem 2. Let t be a positive integer. If a graph G satisfies zig(G) ≥ t, then G
contains K⌊t/2⌋+1 as an odd-minor.
   As one can see from Figure 1, if any one of the known topological lower bounds
for χ(G) attains a value of at least t, also zig(G) is of value at least t, and thus
the statement of Theorem 2 yields the presence of large odd clique minors in all
graphs for which at least one of the topological parameters displayed in the figure
gets large.
   In the results mentioned so far, both for the topological relaxation of Hadwiger’s
and of the Odd Hadwiger’s conjecture, we can only find a clique minor of half
the size that would be guaranteed if the conjectures were true. Motivated by this
gap, in our second main result, we show that the precise statement of Hadwiger’s
conjecture can be recovered when chromatic number is replaced by one of the lower
bounds in Figure 1, namely cd(H):
Theorem 3. Let t be a positive integer. If a graph G admits a Kneser representa-
tion H such that cd(H) ≥ t, then G contains Kt as a minor.
   In particular, this shows that Hadwiger’s conjecture is true for any graph for
which the Dol’nikov-Křı́ž bound provides a tight estimate of its chromatic number
(such as Kneser graphs, for example).
   It would be nice to also get a similar tight result for other parameters in Figure 1,
or to strengthen Theorem 3 to odd-minors. While we did not succeed at this task
in general, we could at least prove that the precise version of Odd Hadwiger’s
conjecture does hold for the most prominent examples of graphs for which the
topological lower bounds are tight, namely for all Kneser and all Schrijver graphs.
Definition 4 (Kneser graph, Schrijver graph, cf. [21]). Let n, k be positive integers
with n ≥ 2k. The Kneser graph K(n, k) is the graph with vertex-set [n]

two k-subsets of [n] = {1, . . . , n} are made adjacent iff they are disjoint. The
Schrijver graph S(n, k) is the subgraph of K(n, k) induced by all those k-subsets of
[n] that are cyclically stable, i.e. that do not contain two consecutive elements in
the natural cyclical ordering of [n].
    Following Lovász proof of Kneser’s conjecture that χ(K(n, k)) = n − 2k + 2,
Schrijver [21] proved that the same tight estimate holds for all Schrijver graphs,
i.e., χ(S(n, k)) = n−2k+2 and that moreover, S(n, k) is minimal with this property,
in that the chromatic number drops if any vertex is removed. The following result
proves the Odd Hadwiger’s conjecture for all Kneser and Schrijver graphs.
Theorem 4. For every choice of positive integers n, k such that n ≥ 2k, the Schri-
jver graph S(n, k) (and thus also the Kneser graph K(n, k)) contains Kn−2k+2 as
an odd-minor.
   We remark that under the restrictive assumption that n − 2k + 2 = t is fixed
as a constant while n and k are sufficiently large as a function of t, the result in
Theorem 4 was already proved by Simonyi and Zsbán [24]. Our result is more
general in that no conditions on n and k are required for Theorem 4 to hold.

                          2. Proofs of Theorems 2 and 3
   In this section, we present the proofs of Theorem 2 and Theorem 3. The proof
of Theorem 2 builds on the following key lemma from [25]. The statement given
here is slightly stronger than the statement of Lemma 4 in [25], but looking at the
proof of Lemma 4 in [25] one readily checks that it does in fact yield Lemma 5 as
stated here.
Lemma 5 (cf. Lemma 4 in [25]). Let G be a graph. There exists n ∈ N and a
partition of V (G) into n non-empty sets X1 , . . . , Xn such that the following holds:
    (1) For every 1 ≤ i ≤ n, the graph G[Xi ] is bipartite and connected.
    (2) For every 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n, and every vertex v ∈ Xj , the following holds.
         Either v has no neighbors in Xi , or it has two distinct neighbors u1 , u2
         where u1 and u2 lie on different sides of the bipartition of G[Xi ].
   To present the proof of Theorem 2, it will be convenient to work with the notion
of odd Kk -expansions from [8], defined as follows.
Definition 5. A Kk -expansion is a graph F that consists of the disjoint union of
k trees T1 , . . . , Tk , together with exactly one edge joining every pair of two distinct
trees. We say that F is an odd Kk -expansion, if there exists a color-assignment
ctree : V (T1 ) ∪ · · · ∪ V (Tk ) → {1, 2} such that for every i = 1, . . . , k, the restriction
of ctree to V (Ti ) forms a proper coloring of the tree Ti , while every edge connecting
two distinct trees is monochromatic with respect to ctree .
    The following fact is folklore, see also [8]:
Observation 1. If G is a graph and k a positive integer, then G contains Kk as an
odd-minor if and only if there exists a subgraph of G that is an odd Kk -expansion.
    We are now ready for the proof of Theorem 2.

   In this section, we give the self-contained proof of Theorem 4. Again, we work
with the notion of odd clique-expansions introduced in the previous section.

show that the Schrijver graph S(n, k) contains Kn−2k+2 as an odd-minor, we will
construct n−2k + 2 vertex-disjoint trees T1 , . . . , Tn−2k , Tn−2k+1 , Tn−2k+2 contained
in S(n, k), and equip each of them with a proper coloring using colors 1 and 2 such
that any pair of trees is joined by at least one monochromatic edge. The reader
is encouraged to look at Figure 2 for an illustration of the somewhat technical
construction.

that no set A ∈ A contains both of the elements 1 and 2k − 1. Moreover, it is easy

colorings both consist of assigning color 1 to their respective vertex.
   We now claim that the collection of trees T1 , . . . , Tn−2k+2 , with each tree equipped
with a proper {1, 2}-coloring as described above, forms an odd Kn−2k+2 -expansion
in the Schrijver graph S(n, k). All that is left to verify here is that (A) all the trees
are pairwise vertex-disjoint and (B) for every two distinct i, j ∈ [n − 2k + 2] there
exists at least one monochromatic edge connecting Ti and Tj .
   To verify (A), first note that the sets {1, 3, . . . , 2k − 1}, {2, 4, . . . , 2k − 2, n}
that form the single vertices of Tn−2k+1 and Tn−2k+2 are not contained in any
of the sets Ai , i = 1, . . . , n − 2k. For {1, 3 . . . , 2k − 1} this follows directly by
definition, since each set in one of the Ai has at most k − 1 elements within [2k],
while {1, 3 . . . , 2k − 1} has k. For {2, 4, . . . , 2k − 2, n} this follows since the only
way a set of the form B ∪ {c(B) + i + (2k − 2)} for some B ∈ A can contain the
element n is if i = n − 2k and c(B) = 2, however, the latter implies 2k − 1 ∈ B, in
contrast to the set {2, 4, . . . , 2k − 2, n}, which does not contain this element. It thus
remains to be shown that Ai ∩ Aj = ∅ for 1 ≤ i < j ≤ n − 2k. Suppose towards a
contradiction that B ∪ {c(B) + i + (2k − 2)} ∈ Aj for some B ∈ A. This implies
that c(B) + j + (2k − 2) = c(B) + i + (2k − 2), a contradiction.
   To verify (B), let any pair {i, j} ⊆ [n − 2k + 2] with i < j be given, and let us
find a monochromatic edge between Ti and Tj . Suppose first j ≤ n − 2k. Then
we have A0 ∪ {i + 2k − 1} ∈ Ai = V (Ti ) and A1 ∪ {j + 2k − 1} ∈ Aj = V (Tj ),
and clearly (A0 ∪ {i + 2k − 1}) ∩ (A1 ∪ {j + 2k − 1}) = ∅. Since the proper
colorings of Ti and Tj assign color 1 to both of these sets, we have found an edge of
S(n, k) going between Ti and Tj that is monochromatic, as desired. Next, suppose
j ∈ {n − 2k + 1, n − 2k + 2} and i ≤ n − 2k. It is then easy to see that the pair of
sets A0 ∪ {i + (2k − 1)}, {1, 3, . . . , 2k − 1} (if j = n − 2k + 1), and the pair of sets
A1 ∪ {i + (2k − 1)}, {2, 4, . . . , 2k − 2, n} (if j = n − 2k + 2) forms a monochromatic
edge between Ti and Tj in this case. Finally, if i = n − 2k + 1, j = n − 2k + 2
the unique edge between Ti and Tj is clearly monochromatic. Finally, this shows
that S(n, k) contains an odd Kn−2k+2 -expansion, and thus contains Kn−2k+2 as an
odd-minor. This concludes the proof.                                                       □
